# Cortisol Face: Is the Trend Real? What the Research Says

"Cortisol face" took off on social media as a name for a puffy or rounder face, blamed on stress. The idea borrows from something real. Very high cortisol over a long time can change how the face looks. But that happens in a medical condition, not from a stressful month.

Here's what's real, what isn't, and when a change in your face is a reason to see a doctor.

*This article is for general information and isn't medical advice.*

## What is cortisol face?

"Cortisol face" isn't a medical term. Online, it usually means a face that looks puffy, swollen or rounder than before, with the claim that stress hormones are the cause.

The real version is called a "round face" or "moon face." It's a recognized sign of Cushing's syndrome, a condition in which the body is exposed to too much cortisol for a long time ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome)).

## Moon face and Cushing's syndrome

Cushing's syndrome is a medical condition that a doctor diagnoses using your medical history, a physical exam and lab tests ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome)). Two things about it matter here.

**The most common cause is medicine.** According to the NIH, the most common cause of Cushing's syndrome is long-term, high-dose use of glucocorticoid medicines, which are cortisol-like steroids such as prednisone ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome); [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000410.htm)). Doctors prescribe these for conditions like asthma, rheumatoid arthritis and lupus. If you take them and notice your face changing, talk to your doctor. Don't stop them on your own.

**When the body makes too much cortisol by itself, it's rare.** The NIH estimates this form affects about 40 to 70 people per million ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome)).

Along with a round face, doctors look for signs like a fatty hump between the shoulders, extra fat at the base of the neck, and other changes together. One sign on its own doesn't mean Cushing's.

## Can everyday stress give you "cortisol face"?

We found no research showing that everyday stress causes a rounder face. Ordinary stress does raise cortisol for a while, but at nothing like the levels and duration seen in Cushing's syndrome. A University of Colorado endocrinologist interviewed about the trend notes that many factors can cause facial swelling, and that everyday cortisol rises with things like stress, illness and the time of day ([CU Anschutz](https://news.cuanschutz.edu/department-of-medicine/cortisol-face)).

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you notice real, lasting changes in your face or body, especially along with any of these:

- A rounder face that doesn't go away
- New fat at the base of the neck or between the shoulders
- Weight gain you can't explain, thin skin, easy bruising, or purple stretch marks ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000410.htm))
- Muscle weakness, high blood pressure or high blood sugar
- You take steroid medicines and have noticed changes

A doctor diagnoses Cushing's syndrome with lab tests. It's not something you can confirm from photos or a symptom list.

## FAQ

**What is cortisol face?**
A social-media name for a puffy or rounder face blamed on stress. The medical version, "moon face," is a sign of Cushing's syndrome, a condition a doctor diagnoses.

**What does high cortisol face look like?**
In Cushing's syndrome, the face becomes rounder and fuller, usually alongside other signs. Puffiness that comes and goes is different.

**Is moon face caused by cortisol?**
Yes, in Cushing's syndrome, where cortisol or steroid medicine levels stay very high over time. Everyday stress hasn't been shown to cause it.

## The short version

"Cortisol face" borrows from a real sign of Cushing's syndrome, a rare condition that's most often caused by steroid medicine. No research shows everyday stress gives you a rounder face. Lasting changes to your face are a reason to see a doctor, not to try a trend.
