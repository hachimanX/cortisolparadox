# Why Is Cortisol High in the Morning? Your Body's Daily Rhythm Explained

Cortisol is often called the "stress hormone," so it can be unsettling to learn that it's highest first thing in the morning, before anything stressful has happened. But that morning high is normal. It's part of a daily rhythm that helps you wake up and get going.

Here's how the cortisol curve works, why it peaks in the morning, and a recent study that changes how scientists think about that peak.

*This article is for general information and isn't medical advice.*

## Is cortisol high in the morning?

Yes. In most people, cortisol starts rising in the early hours and peaks shortly after their usual wake time. It then falls through the day and is low by the time they go to sleep. A master clock in the brain drives this pattern ([O'Byrne et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35128146/); [University of Bristol, 2025](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2025/january/waking-stress.html)). The NIH notes that cortisol production normally drops just after we fall asleep ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome)).

## The cortisol curve, hour by hour

Here's the typical shape, in words:

- **Second half of the night:** Cortisol starts to climb while you're still asleep.
- **Around waking:** It reaches its daily peak.
- **Through the day:** It falls gradually.
- **Late evening and early night:** It's at its lowest.

The exact timing shifts with your sleep schedule and with age. In a study of 143 healthy adults, older age raised cortisol in the late evening and early night and moved the daily peak earlier, by about 24 minutes per decade ([Roelfsema et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28760748/)).

## Why cortisol is high in the morning

The morning rise lines up with the time your body is getting ready to be active. It's part of the clock-driven rhythm, not a sign that you're stressed.

That's why a high morning reading isn't a problem in itself. It's also why the time of day matters so much when cortisol is measured.

## The "cortisol awakening response," and a study that questions it

For years, researchers have described a sharp rise in cortisol over the first 30 to 45 minutes after waking, called the cortisol awakening response ([Stalder et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26563991/)). It's widely measured in stress research, usually with saliva samples people take after they wake.

A 2025 study from the University of Bristol questioned what that rise means. The team used automated sampling to measure cortisol in 201 healthy adults at home, both before and after they woke. The rate of increase in the hour after waking was no different from the hour before. The authors suggest the after-waking rise mostly reflects the normal daily curve that was already under way, rather than a separate response to waking ([University of Bristol, 2025](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2025/january/waking-stress.html); published in *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* as [Klaas et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809311/)).

That's one study, and it won't settle the question alone. But it's a reason to be careful with claims that your "morning cortisol spike" is something you need to manage.

## Does coffee or the way you wake up change it?

Caffeine can raise cortisol in people who aren't used to it, but daily coffee drinkers adapt, and their first morning cup may not raise it at all ([Lovallo et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16204431/)). We cover this in [coffee and cortisol](/guides/coffee-and-cortisol/).

## When to see a doctor

A single cortisol reading only means something next to the time it was taken and the reason for the test. Talk to a doctor if:

- You're exhausted most mornings for weeks, even after enough sleep.
- You have unexplained weight changes, a rounder face, muscle weakness, dizziness or low blood pressure.
- You've had a cortisol test and aren't sure what the result means.
- You take steroid medicines.

A doctor can order the right test at the right time of day and explain the result.

## FAQ

**Why is cortisol highest in the morning?**
Your body clock drives cortisol up during the second half of the night so it peaks around waking. That helps you get going for the day.

**Is high cortisol in the morning bad?**
No. A morning peak is normal. Whether a level is too high depends on the time it was measured and the test used, which is a question for a doctor.

**What is the cortisol curve?**
It's the daily pattern of cortisol: rising in the second half of the night, peaking around waking, falling through the day, and lowest in the late evening.

## The short version

Cortisol is high in the morning because your body clock is designed that way. A 2025 study suggests waking itself may not cause an extra spike. A morning peak is normal, and questions about your own levels belong with a doctor.
