# Food and Cortisol: What Research Says About Eating Patterns and Stress

Search for cortisol and food and you'll find long lists: "cortisol triggering foods" to avoid and "foods that lower cortisol" to eat. Very few of those lists cite a study. When you look at the research, two drinks have reasonably clear effects on cortisol. The evidence for whole eating patterns is real but small. And most of the single "superfoods" haven't been tested in people in any meaningful way.

Here's what the studies show.

*This article is for general information and isn't medical advice.*

## Are there cortisol triggering foods?

The two best-studied ones are drinks.

**Caffeine.** In a controlled trial, caffeine clearly raised cortisol in people who'd avoided it for five days. In people taking it every day, the morning response disappeared, though an afternoon rise remained ([Lovallo et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16204431/)). We cover this in [coffee and cortisol](/guides/coffee-and-cortisol/).

**Alcohol.** In the Whitehall II study of more than 3,600 adults, cortisol in men rose with weekly alcohol intake, and heavy drinkers had a flatter daily cortisol curve ([Badrick et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18073316/)). That was a cross-sectional study, so it shows a link, not cause and effect. Alcohol also disrupts sleep in the second half of the night ([Ebrahim et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347102/)).

We didn't find good human evidence that sugar, dairy, gluten or other specific foods "trigger" cortisol in everyday eating.

## Foods that lower cortisol: what the evidence shows

| Claim | What the research shows | How strong |
|---|---|---|
| A Mediterranean-style diet lowers cortisol | In an 18-month trial of 294 adults, two Mediterranean diet groups had lower fasting morning cortisol than at the start, but the drop was small (under 2%), only one group beat the standard healthy-diet group, there was no change at 6 months, and most participants were men on calorie-reduced diets ([Alufer et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38034010/)) | Small effect; one trial |
| Omega-3 fats lower the stress response | In a 4-month trial of 138 adults, the higher-dose omega-3 group had about 19% lower overall cortisol around a stress test than placebo ([Madison et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33875799/)). It's one trial, and it tested supplements, not food | One trial |
| Dark chocolate, tea or a single "superfood" lowers cortisol | We didn't find a systematic review of human trials showing a meaningful effect on cortisol | Weak |
| Cutting out caffeine and alcohol | Both have clearer links to cortisol or sleep than any single food | Moderate |

The pattern is that the more specific the food claim, the weaker the evidence behind it.

## What may make sense on the plate

Without promising any effect on cortisol, a few things are reasonable:

- **Regular meals.** Eating at fairly regular times fits with a steady daily rhythm. This is our reading, not a tested cortisol rule.
- **Balanced plates.** Protein, vegetables, slower carbs and some healthy fat. This is general healthy-eating advice, not a way to lower cortisol.
- **Watch caffeine timing and alcohol.** These have the clearest evidence.

## When to see a doctor

Talk to a doctor if:

- You're thinking about a big change in diet and you have diabetes, kidney disease or another long-term condition.
- You've had unexplained weight change or blood sugar problems.
- You find it hard to cut back on alcohol.
- You're worried about your eating habits or your relationship with food.

## FAQ

**What foods cause high cortisol?**
Caffeine (in people not used to it) and alcohol have the clearest links. We didn't find good evidence that specific foods like sugar or dairy raise cortisol in everyday eating.

**What foods lower cortisol?**
No single food has been shown to lower cortisol in a meaningful way. A long Mediterranean diet trial found a very small drop in morning cortisol after 18 months.

**What are high cortisol foods to avoid?**
There's no proven list. Limiting late caffeine and alcohol is the best-supported change.

## The short version

Caffeine and alcohol have the clearest links to cortisol. Whole eating patterns show small effects at best, and most single-food claims are untested. Regular, balanced meals make sense for health in general. For the bigger picture, see [wired but tired](/guides/wired-but-tired/).
