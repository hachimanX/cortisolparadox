# At-Home Cortisol Tests: What They Measure and Their Limits

At-home cortisol tests promise to tell you whether your stress hormone is "too high." They can measure cortisol. But a cortisol number on its own means very little, and diagnosing a real cortisol problem takes more than one kit.

Here's what these tests measure, where they fall short, and when to go to a doctor instead.

*This article is for general information and isn't medical advice.*

## How to test cortisol levels at home

Most home kits use one of three samples:

- **Saliva.** You spit into a tube, sometimes several times across a day. Saliva reflects the "free" (active) cortisol in your body ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/cortisol-test/); [El-Farhan et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28068807/)).
- **Urine.** Some kits use dried urine samples collected at set times through the day. A small study run by a testing lab found dried and liquid urine gave very similar cortisol results ([Newman et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33354516/)).
- **Blood spot.** A finger-prick sample dried on a card, which can be collected at home and mailed to a lab ([Olthof et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38874994/)).

You send the sample to a lab and get a report back, often with a chart of your cortisol across the day.

## Why one cortisol number means so little

Cortisol changes a lot through the day. It normally starts rising in the early hours, peaks shortly after waking and drops after you fall asleep ([O'Byrne et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35128146/); [University of Bristol, 2025](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2025/january/waking-stress.html); [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome)). So the same number can be normal in the morning and high at night. That makes the time of the sample critical. We explain the daily curve in [why cortisol is high in the morning](/guides/why-is-cortisol-high-in-the-morning/).

Other things also change results: stress on the day, illness, sleep, exercise, some medicines, and pregnancy. MedlinePlus notes that your provider may ask you to stop certain medicines, including estrogen, before a blood cortisol test ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003693.htm)). Kit instructions usually also tell you when not to eat or drink before collecting.

## How doctors test for a cortisol problem

Doctors test for cortisol problems differently from home kits. To check for Cushing's syndrome, the Endocrine Society's guideline recommends starting with one test that has high accuracy, such as a late-night saliva test, a 24-hour urine test or a dexamethasone suppression test. If that result is abnormal, people should see an endocrinologist and have a second test ([Nieman et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18334580/)).

The guideline also says doctors should first rule out steroid medicines as the cause, and should test people who have several features of the condition that are getting worse. So doctors don't test cortisol just because someone feels stressed. They test when there's a clinical reason, and they repeat it.

| Question | Home kit | Doctor's testing |
|---|---|---|
| Can it measure cortisol? | Yes | Yes |
| Can it diagnose Cushing's or adrenal insufficiency? | No | Yes, with repeat and confirmatory tests |
| Does it account for medicines and timing? | Partly, if you follow instructions | Yes |
| Who interprets it? | A report | An endocrinologist, if needed |

## What a home test might be useful for

If you're curious about your daily pattern, a well-timed home kit can show it. But it can't tell you whether you have a medical condition, and an "abnormal" result is a reason to see a doctor, not a diagnosis. A "normal" result also doesn't rule out a problem if you have symptoms.

We didn't find independent studies checking the accuracy of specific consumer kits, so we can't say how they compare with each other.

## When to see a doctor instead

See a doctor, rather than relying on a home kit, if you have:

- A rounder face, fat between the shoulders, thin skin, easy bruising or wide purple stretch marks ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000410.htm))
- High blood pressure or high blood sugar that's new or hard to control
- Weakness, dizziness, weight loss or low blood pressure
- Long-term use of steroid medicines
- A home test result that worries you

## FAQ

**Are at-home cortisol tests accurate?**
They can measure cortisol, but accuracy depends on timing, handling and the lab, and we didn't find independent checks of specific kits. They can't diagnose a medical condition.

**Can I test my cortisol at home?**
Yes. But one result means little without the time it was taken and a doctor to interpret it.

**What's the best cortisol test?**
It depends on the question a doctor is trying to answer. For Cushing's syndrome, guidelines recommend late-night saliva, 24-hour urine or a dexamethasone test, followed by a second test if the first is abnormal.

## The short version

Home cortisol kits measure a number, but cortisol shifts through the day and with many other factors. Diagnosis needs the right test at the right time, repeated, and read by a doctor.
