# Cortisol Supplements: An Evidence Check on the Claims

"Supplements to lower cortisol" is one of the biggest searches in this area, and most of the pages that rank for it sell supplements. We don't sell any, and we don't recommend brands or doses. This is a check of what the research shows for the most common ones, and what it doesn't.

One fact first: in the US, the FDA doesn't approve dietary supplements for safety or effectiveness before they're sold ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/dietarysupplements/usingdietarysupplements/)). A label saying "supports healthy cortisol" hasn't been checked by the FDA.

*This article is for general information and isn't medical advice. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before taking any supplement.*

## The evidence, supplement by supplement

| Supplement | What the research shows | How strong |
|---|---|---|
| Ashwagandha | A 2025 meta-analysis of randomized trials found lower cortisol with ashwagandha, but no significant change in how stressed people said they felt ([Albalawi, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40746175/)). Trials are mostly short | Moderate for cortisol in short trials; mixed for how people feel |
| Magnesium | One small analysis found lower urinary cortisol over 24 weeks. A review of 18 studies on anxiety rated the evidence as poor quality. See [magnesium and cortisol](/guides/magnesium-and-cortisol/) | Weak |
| Omega-3 | A 4-month trial of 138 adults found about 19% lower overall cortisol around a stress test in the higher-dose group ([Madison et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33875799/)) | One trial |
| Rhodiola | We didn't find a systematic review showing a consistent effect on cortisol | Weak |
| Phosphatidylserine | We didn't find a systematic review showing a consistent effect on cortisol | Weak |

## Ashwagandha: the most studied, and the most caveats

Ashwagandha has the most trials behind it. But there are real limits:

- **Short trials.** Most are short. NIH says ashwagandha may be safe for up to about 3 months, but its long-term safety isn't known ([NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha)).
- **Lower cortisol isn't the same as feeling better.** The 2025 meta-analysis found lower cortisol without a significant change in perceived stress.
- **Safety.** The NIH's LiverTox database lists ashwagandha as a cause of clinically apparent liver injury, based on case reports, usually 2 to 12 weeks after starting ([LiverTox, NIH](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548536/)). LiverTox rates ashwagandha a likely cause (likelihood score B). Cases are rare, and most resolved after stopping, but a few were severe.
- **Not for everyone.** People who are pregnant, have thyroid or autoimmune conditions, have liver problems, or take sedatives or other medicines should talk to a doctor first ([NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha)).

## What this means

A supplement that lowers a cortisol reading in a short trial isn't the same as one that makes you feel less stressed, sleep better or stay healthier. Most of these products haven't been tested on those outcomes over the long term. If your cortisol is truly high, the cause needs a doctor, not a supplement. If you're just stressed and tired, habits like regular movement, slow breathing and a calmer evening have research behind them too, and come with fewer safety questions. ## When to see a doctor

Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before taking a cortisol supplement if you:

- Take any prescription medicines
- Are pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive
- Have liver, thyroid, kidney or autoimmune conditions
- Have symptoms you think are caused by high cortisol

Stop the supplement and get medical help if you notice yellowing skin or eyes, dark urine, itching or pain in the upper right of your belly.

## FAQ

**What are the best cortisol supplements?**
We don't rank them, because no supplement has strong long-term evidence. Ashwagandha has the most short-term trials, along with real safety concerns.

**Do supplements to lower cortisol work?**
Some lower cortisol readings in short trials. Evidence on whether people feel less stressed is mixed, and long-term health effects haven't been studied.

**Are cortisol supplements safe?**
Not always. Supplements aren't FDA-approved before sale, and ashwagandha has been linked to rare cases of liver injury. Ask a doctor or pharmacist first.

## The short version

Most cortisol supplements have weak or short-term evidence. Ashwagandha has the most, with real caveats on safety and on whether people feel better. Talk to a doctor before taking any of them. For the bigger picture on stress and tiredness, see [wired but tired](/guides/wired-but-tired/).
